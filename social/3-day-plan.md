6. Text card: Why it mattered.

Caption:

Before Raja Ravi Varma, most Indians had never seen a face for Shakuntala, Damayanti or Draupadi. He gave them one.

Born in 1848 into a royal family in Kilimanoor, Kerala, he taught himself European oil painting and used it to retell the Mahabharata, the Ramayana and the plays of Kalidasa. In 1894 he set up his own press and printed his gods and heroines as affordable oleographs, and those pictures reached homes across the country.

## Day 2: Single post, Painting of the day

Slides (1):
1. Girl with a Pearl Earring (Johannes Vermeer, c. 1665). Mauritshuis, The Hague. Oil on canvas.

Caption:

It is not a portrait. In Vermeer's Delft, a picture like this was called a tronie: a study of a face or an expression, not of one particular person. We still do not know who she was.

Look at the turban. That blue is natural ultramarine, ground from lapis lazuli and as costly as gold in the 1600s. And the famous pearl? A few strokes of white and grey paint. There is no hook and no string. Your eye supplies the rest.

Vermeer left only about thirty-five paintings. This one hangs in the Mauritshuis in The Hague.

What do you notice first, the earring or the eyes?
