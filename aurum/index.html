Spotlight · India

Bharat Gallery

❦

From the lamp-lit murals of the Ajanta caves to the miniature courts of the Mughals and Rajputs, India has one of the world's oldest and richest painting traditions.

Raja Ravi Varma (1848–1906), a prince of Kilimanoor in Kerala, taught himself European oil painting and brought it to the stories of the Mahabharata, the Ramayana and Kalidasa. When he set up his own press and printed his gods and heroines as affordable oleographs, he gave millions of Indians a shared picture of their myths.

Then came the Bengal School of Abanindranath Tagore and Nandalal Bose, and the luminous village women of Amrita Sher-Gil — the story of how India found its own modern eye.
