About the works shown

Srjan is a gallery made to help people look closely at art, spaces and design, and to learn the story behind each work. We are an independent project and we are not affiliated with any museum, collection, artist, estate or institution named on this site.

The works shown here come from three sources. First, works that are in the public domain, because the period of copyright has ended. Second, photographs that their makers have released under free licences, mostly through Wikimedia Commons. Third, works that living creators have given us permission to show.

Each work remains the property of its museum, owner, collection or creator. Showing a work here does not give you any right to copy, sell or reuse it.

Copyright in India

Srjan is run from India, so we follow the Indian Copyright Act, 1957. Under Section 22 of the Act, copyright in a published artistic work lasts for the life of the creator and sixty years more, counted from the first day of January of the year after the creator dies. When that period ends, the work is free for everyone to use.

We show a work from the catalogue only when its period of copyright has ended, or when we have permission. Works by creators whose copyright has not yet ended are held back automatically, and they will appear on the first day of January of the year when their copyright ends.

The Act allows some uses of protected works without permission, such as fair dealing for private study, research, criticism and review, and copying by a teacher or pupil during instruction (Section 52). These exceptions are narrow, and we do not rely on them to show a work to the public. Section 52 also allows a photograph of a work of architecture to be made and published, and this is why buildings can be shown.

Even after copyright ends, the creator keeps the moral right to be named as the creator. This is why every work on Srjan carries the name of its creator.

A photograph of a building, an object or a room can have a copyright of its own, and the photographer's licence is listed in the credits below. For photographs of flat works such as paintings, we follow the licence that Wikimedia Commons states for the file.

Accuracy

We take care to name the right creator for every work and to tell its story correctly. The stories are written for general readers, and some facts, such as dates, measurements and traditional attributions, are still discussed by scholars. Where a creator is traditionally credited but not certain, we say so. Some craft traditions and house forms are shown by one example, which stands for the tradition and not for a single maker.

The site is provided as it is, for learning and enjoyment. It is not a source for valuation, authentication, purchase or legal decisions.

Corrections and removal

If you are a creator, an owner or a rights holder and you believe something here is wrong or should not be shown, please write to us through our Instagram page, @srjan.co. Tell us which work you mean and what you would like us to do. We will act on your message promptly.

Privacy

We collect the details you enter in the application form: your name, email, phone number (if given), city and country, biography, links, portrait and artwork files. Your email address and phone number are used only to contact you about your application and any enquiries, and are never shown publicly. Your name, location, biography, portrait, statement and artworks are shown publicly if your exhibition is approved.

Enquiries from visitors (name, email and message) are shared with the relevant artist or handled by Srjan on their behalf. We count visits to the site in total and do not use advertising trackers. The works you save are kept only in your own browser.

The site loads its fonts from Google Fonts, which means your device contacts Google when a page opens. You can ask to see, correct or delete your data at any time by writing to us through our Instagram page.
